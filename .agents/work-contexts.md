# Work Contexts

**This file ships empty on purpose. Fill it in before you do client work.**

If you operate under more than one commercial identity — your own clients, plus
a white-label partner, plus an agency you subcontract to — this is where the
boundaries between them get written down, and where an agent looks before
routing a call, a project, or an invoice.

Getting this wrong is one of the few genuinely expensive errors an agent can
make here: work attributed to the wrong entity, a client name mentioned to the
wrong partner, a rate quoted from the wrong agreement. Names alone are not
enough to disambiguate, because the same people show up across contexts.

If everything you do is under one banner, write one line saying so and move on.

---

## Contexts

For each one, record:

- **Name and what it is.** Your own company, a partnership, a white-label
  arrangement, an employer.
- **Who is in it.** Partners, counterparts, the people whose presence on a call
  is a reliable signal — and, importantly, the people who appear across
  contexts and are therefore *not* a signal.
- **What kind of work runs through it.** Industries, service lines, deal sizes.
- **How money works.** Who bills, who pays you, revenue share, rate card.
- **What must never cross over.** Client lists, pricing, tooling, data.

### Example shape

> **Acme Partners** — white-label delivery. I run build and implementation for
> their clients under their brand. Counterpart: [name]. Their clients are not
> my accounts and must never be approached directly. They bill the client; I
> invoice Acme monthly.
>
> Reliable signal: the client-side contact names. **Not** a reliable signal:
> [counterpart], who appears on calls across all three contexts.

---

## Routing rules

Write the actual decision procedure an agent should follow when a new call,
project, or document shows up and the context is ambiguous.

The rule that matters most: **when genuinely ambiguous, ask — never guess.**
A misrouted project is far more expensive to unwind than a question.
